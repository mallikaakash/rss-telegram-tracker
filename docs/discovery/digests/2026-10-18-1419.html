Echo: Error-Compensated 4-Bit Tensor-ParallelCommunication for LLM Training and Inference

A high-signal ML systems paper solving the unhideable all-reduce bottleneck in tensor parallelism using error-compensated 4-bit communication without specialized FP4 hardware.
  • Applies error-feedback compensation to tensor-parallel all-reduce, moving 4-bit data while summing in high precision to prove depth-independent bounded error.
  • Achieves 2.09x speedup over 16-bit NCCL on 1GB transfers on A100s, yielding up to 1.44x TTFT speedup under network contention.
  • Maintains benchmark accuracy within 0.4 points of FP16 on Qwen3-8B and stays within 0.9% of baseline loss during pretraining.

FreeToken: 284B on a Gaming Desktop, 753B on One Workstation GPU - and the Ratio That Makes It Work | Fodev JEO

A thoughtful practitioner breakdown of the FreeToken edge MoE serving architecture, analyzing cache miss handling and PCIe residency constraints from a game engine perspective.
  • Explores how to serve 284B to 753B parameter MoE models on consumer GPUs by managing the 140 GB parameter footprint across host RAM and PCIe.
  • Highlights the challenge of prefill sparsity collapse and the difficulty of handling decode cache misses within CUDA Graphs.
  • Bridges high-level distributed systems theory with low-level game-engine-style memory residency management.

No Single Tokenizer Feature Reliably Predicts Downstream Language Model Performance

A rigorous empirical study across 200 models and 9 languages that systematically debunks the predictive utility of standard intrinsic tokenizer evaluations.
  • Evaluates approximately 200 language models across 9 languages to correlate intrinsic tokenizer metrics with downstream performance.
  • Finds that no single intrinsic tokenizer metric reliably predicts downstream task accuracy across diverse model families and languages.
  • Demonstrates the necessity of empirical pretraining benchmarks over static vocabulary heuristics.
