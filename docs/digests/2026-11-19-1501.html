This featherweight, flying robot carries its own sensors and computers

  • Over the past decade, MIT researchers have developed aerial microrobots with bumblebee-like speed, agility, and durability, for tasks like precision agriculture or search-and-rescue. But these featherweight microrobots couldn’t carry the electronics needed for navigation, forcing them to remain connected to a motion-capture system in the lab. Now, the researchers have designed a flapping-wing microrobot with onboard sensors and computation, which can follow a complex trajectory and land precisely on a sunflower outside the lab’s motion capture environment.
